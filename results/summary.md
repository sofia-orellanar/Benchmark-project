# Results Summary

Final evaluation metrics for all 4 assembly conditions (MEGAHIT, SPAdes, each with and without redundans post-processing), generated by the pipeline in `scripts/snakefile`. Assembly and redundans steps were run in triplicate to measure runtime/memory variance; the metrics below are from the representative `rep1` assembly of each condition (assembly content is deterministic given the same input and tool version). Full QUAST and BUSCO output directories are not committed due to size. These numbers were pulled directly from `results/quast/*/report.tsv` and `results/busco/*/short_summary*.txt`.

Genome fraction and misassembly counts were computed against both parent-strain references (S288C and YJM789) separately, since the sequenced genome is a diploid hybrid of the two; the values above are averaged where the two references gave similar results (see individual `report.tsv` files for the full breakdown per reference).

## Key findings

**1. redundans substantially reduced MEGAHIT's redundant contigs, with little effect on SPAdes.**
redundans cut MEGAHIT's contig count by 4.4× (2,127 → 477) and dropped its duplication ratio to near 1.0 (1.015 → 1.005), consistent with collapsing heterozygous haplotig pairs as designed. SPAdes's duplication ratio was already close to 1.0 before redundans ran (~1.019), so redundans had comparatively little to correct. SPAdes's assembly problem (see below) was not primarily heterozygosity-driven duplication.

**2. SPAdes was dramatically more fragmented than MEGAHIT on this dataset.**
SPAdes (run with `--isolate`, per its own recommendation for high-coverage isolate data) produced 68,936 contigs with an N50 of only 1,303 bp and 44.1% BUSCO completeness, compared to MEGAHIT's 2,127 contigs, N50 of 84,052 bp, and 99.3% completeness. This result was confirmed reproducible across two independent full pipeline runs (identical output down to the exact BUSCO stop-codon counts), ruling out a transient pipeline error.

**Working hypothesis:** `--isolate` mode disables SPAdes's BayesHammer read error-correction step by design (confirmed expected behavior per the SPAdes development team, not a bug). This dataset has two unusual properties that likely interact badly with skipping error correction: very high raw coverage (~200×+, not subsampled) and a highly variable insert size (222 ± 134 bp, flagged by SPAdes itself during the run). Without error correction, sequencing errors at this depth likely inflated the assembly graph with spurious branches, and the unstable insert size likely further hindered repeat resolution. Together plausibly explaining the severe fragmentation. This was not tested directly (e.g., by rerunning without `--isolate`) due to time constraints; it is presented here as the most consistent explanation given SPAdes's own logged warnings, not as a confirmed mechanism.
